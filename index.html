Viscoelastic Haemostatic Assays

Standard clotting screens (INR/APTT) only measure the time to the start of fibrin formation. TEG measures the entire lifespan of a clot: from formation, through strength (platelet-fibrin interaction), to degradation (fibrinolysis). This guides massive transfusion accurately.

The Physics: Top-Down View

Phase 1: Fluid
Initial state.
Torsion (Trace)

1 Fluid Phase (R-Time)

The Cup (Outer) oscillates 4°45' every 10s. The blood is liquid. Red cells flow around the stationary Pin (Inner). No torque is transferred. Trace is flat.

2 Clot Initiation (Alpha)

Enzymatic cascade triggers. Fibrin strands (White) grow from the cup wall to the pin, creating a physical tether.

3 Max Strength (MA)

Platelets (Yellow) bind to fibrin, contracting the clot. The clot is now stiff. The Pin is dragged forcefully by the Cup. Max trace amplitude.

4 Lysis (LY30)

Plasmin cuts the fibrin strands. The tether breaks. The Pin loses momentum and returns to stationary, while the Cup continues to oscillate.
